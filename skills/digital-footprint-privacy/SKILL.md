# Find and remove the user’s own exposed personal information with verifiable follow-up

## Operational procedure

1. Confirm ownership and scope: identifiers, country, allowed services and visibility preferences. Search only authorized personal exposure; avoid collecting unrelated family/person data. Mask sensitive identifiers in reports and never commit them to a public repository.
2. Build a source ledger: exact URL, exposed fields, observation date, identity-match confidence and evidence. Distinguish confirmed match, plausible match and unrelated person; a search snippet alone is not proof.
3. Remove or restrict the original page first where possible; then request search-result refresh/removal and data-broker opt-out through the actual supported process. Search deindexing does not delete the source. Procedures differ by country and service; verify current official instructions before legal advice.
4. Draft specific requests with minimum identity information. Inspect recipients, form requirements and authorization before submission. Never treat green checkmarks or a drafted email as a submitted or successful request. Preserve confirmation/receipt and later verify the page while signed out.
5. Review phone-number discoverability, contact upload, profile links, recovery identifiers and app privacy settings. Number aliases can reduce linkability but do not confer anonymity; SMS and mobile-network security claims need independent evidence.
6. Schedule recurring checks only when requested and actually supported. Track submitted, acknowledged, removed, rejected and reappeared states with timestamps. Respect the user’s existing pause on outgoing messages and external-action permissions.

**Check:** A broker’s unreachable page yields “unverified,” not “removed.” An opted-out record that reappears remains an unresolved case. Keep source deletion, deindexing and privacy-setting changes separate.
