# Build, challenge and communicate arguments under evidence and dialogue constraints

## Operational procedure

1. Establish the motion, audience, format, stakes and burden of proof. Distinguish factual, causal, normative, definitional and policy disputes; agree what observation would change the conclusion. Clarify whether the goal is truth-seeking, preparation, moderation or persuasive delivery.
2. Map each important argument as claim → grounds → warrant → qualifier → rebuttal. Separate validity from soundness; map necessary/sufficient conditions, quantifiers, base rates, denominators and counterfactuals. For policy compare realistic alternatives under the same constraints, including doing nothing.
3. Steelman the strongest opposing position before attacking it. Identify the precise crux: evidence, values, definitions, causal model or decision threshold. Generate one counterexample and one discriminating question per pivotal claim; update if they succeed.
4. Audit source independence, quotation context, absolute versus relative risk, selection bias, Simpson’s paradox, ecological inference and correlation/causation. A causal story needs a mechanism plus discriminating evidence, not merely plausibility.
5. Detect traps by explaining the broken inference: equivocation, loaded questions, false dilemmas, straw man, moving goalposts, motte-and-bailey, circularity, special pleading, cherry-picking, false balance, appeal to popularity/authority, tu quoque, ad hominem, post hoc, slippery slope and gish gallop. A named fallacy is a diagnostic cue, not proof that the conclusion is false. Do not answer a loaded premise as if established; split the question and test its presupposition.
6. Handle a flood of claims by selecting the two decisive ones, requesting evidence and preserving an unanswered-claims ledger. For cross-examination ask short, answerable questions about definitions, evidence and the shared crux; avoid humiliation, invented quotations or exploiting personal vulnerabilities.
7. Build a concise constructive case: thesis, strongest reasons, best evidence, acknowledged limitation and strongest objection answered fairly. Distinguish concession of a point from concession of the motion. Adapt pacing, examples and technical detail to the audience without hiding uncertainty.
8. Run a red-team pass: swap sides, check double standards, try a countermodel and identify the strongest honest response. Use an independent reviewer only when disagreement can change the result. Judge evidence/logic separately from rhetorical polish and audience reaction.

**Check:** A response to “everyone uses it, therefore it is safe” explains why prevalence cannot establish safety, requests outcome evidence and does not infer that popularity proves danger. A rebuttal must address the strongest real claim and state what could reverse its conclusion.

**Output when useful:** motion; argument map; strongest countercase; decisive crux; 2–4 cross-examination questions; concise response. Do not force this template on a simple question.

**Limits:** No finite catalog covers every rhetorical tactic. Debate skill cannot guarantee victory or make unsupported claims true.
